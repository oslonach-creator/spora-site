One log, three kinds of grower.

Spora shows as much as you need. Pick Simple or Full when you start and switch whenever you like; nothing is lost either way.

  • Starting with a kit

    Add the kit in front of you and Spora counts the days, tells you when to look and what it should look like, in plain words. Six photo steps take you from opening the box to the first harvest.

  • Serious hobby growers

    Plates, liquid culture, grain and bulk with every parent recorded. Colonies and sectors marked on a photo of the plate, transfer counts, contamination with the cause, and the numbers to compare branches and recipes.

  • Small farms with staff

    Labels and a scanner on every shelf, harvests weighed by the crate, weekly restaurant orders, invoices and a recall report. Everyone on the team sees their own day.

From spore to restaurant crate.

Agar, liquid culture, grain, bulk and fruiting, in one record that follows the work. The screens below are from the app, shown with a sample farm.

Every batch, back to its source.

Each plate, syringe, jar and block gets a permanent code and remembers what it was made from, even when spawn is pooled from several parents. The family tree shows the whole line, and the way back to where it began.

When something goes wrong, Spora flags what was made from the same parent. Quarantined material is held, so it cannot be used by mistake.

Colonisation, logged.

Log how far the mycelium has spread and the picture follows, step by step. Health, the next check and the expected ready date sit on the same page. Jars from one batch share a row, so a shelf of fifty stays readable.

Fruiting, step by step.

Log how far the flush has come and the picture moves with it, every five percent: first knots, pins, young caps, ready to pick. Oysters, lion's mane, king oyster, shiitake, chestnut, pioppino, nameko, enoki and button each grow in their own way, and every step has a short tip on what to look for and when to pick. Labels from a pocket printer.

Small QR labels on a cheap Bluetooth printer such as the Phomemo M221 or a NIIMBOT, one label or a whole tray. Any other printer gets an A4 sheet as a PDF.

One scan, the next step.

Scan a label to open the jar, log a check, harvest it or print it again. Walk the day's checks with the scanner, jar after jar, and each one is logged as you go.

Your week, laid out.

What to inoculate, move, check or harvest, and on which day. The planner works back from the harvest date through agar, liquid culture, grain and bulk. Add your own tasks and get one reminder in the morning.

Genetics, marked on the plate.

From a spore swab to a culture that grows evenly. Mark what you see on a photo of the dish, take daughters from it, and let your own records tell the branches apart.

Draw on the photo of your plate.

Hold the dish inside the guide circle and Spora crops and centres the picture. Draw each colony as a circle and each sector as a dashed wedge. A sector sits inside its colony, and Spora suggests which one from the overlap. Every region has its own colour, and the outlines hide with one tap when you want to look at the mycelium.

Letters start at A on every plate, and the code carries the plate: colony A on MYC-080-P04 is MYC-080-P04A, and its first transfer MYC-080-P04A-T1.

Careful by design: a colony or sector is a place on a plate and what you saw there. A daughter taken from it is a candidate isolate, not proof of a pure or better genotype.

Plates from A, B and C at once.

Choose how many plates or slants to take from each colony or sector. Spora makes them in one step, gives each its code and its own candidate branch, and the labels are ready to print.

Branches, side by side.

Two to four branches from the same source, side by side: days to colonise, contamination, yield and biological efficiency. Every number shows how many batches it rests on, and nothing is estimated.

Photo history
Every photo of a plate by day, tied to the colony or sector it shows. Put any two side by side to see what changed.
Transfers counted, not guessed
T counts agar transfers and G counts grain to grain. The F-generation is never changed by a transfer; you set it when there is a documented cross.
Your own codes
Write the code from your marker pen next to the permanent one on any plate, jar or bag, and search by either.

The records behind every jar.

What a mycologist wants written down, in words a first-year grower can follow.

Contamination, with the cause
Mark a jar fine, suspect or contaminated, with a photo and the likely cause. The built-in guide helps you tell cobweb from healthy mycelium before you throw out a jar that was fine.
Sterilisation runs
Time at pressure, temperature, what went in and how many containers. Every jar points back to its run, so a bad cook shows up in the losses.
Where losses come from
Losses by cause, spawn source, sterilisation run, recipe, room and shelf, with the count behind every percentage.
Yields per flush
Each flush weighed and numbered for you, with biological efficiency, so strains and recipes are compared on numbers rather than memory.
Culture bank
Slants, master plates and liquid culture: where each one is, when to check it, and a warning before you use up the last living culture of a strain.
Supplies and recipes
Grain, bags and supplement with days left and what to buy soon. Recipes carry their cost, so every batch has one.

For farms that deliver every week.

Your restaurants and what each orders every week, in kilos per mushroom with prices from your price list, set against what is ready in the cold room. Two weeks of deliveries, packed oldest first, so you see what is short before the day comes.

A page for each restaurant
Contact details, the weekly order by day, what is coming up, the last deliveries and open invoices, on one page.
Harvest by scan or from the list
Scan the label and weigh. No label? Pick the batch from a list or type the code written on the jar. A harvest session weighs a whole room on a big number pad.
Cold room and stock
Picked lots, oldest first, with days left. Deliveries take from it and the ledger keeps up.
Cost per kilo
Materials, energy and labour per batch, rolled up into the cost per kilo by strain and recipe.
Invoices and delivery notes
Bill a restaurant for what went out, VAT per line, as a PDF, and mark it paid. Export the sales for your accounts.
Recall report
For any delivery or batch: where it came from, any problems on the way, and every customer it went to. PDF or CSV.

Your records, kept where you choose.

Spora keeps everything on the phone first, so it works in a basement with no signal. Then you decide what leaves the phone.

    Nothing is sent anywhere, even when you are signed in. A weekly reminder helps you save a backup to Files or Drive.

    Records sync between your phones and your team, stored in the EU. Strains and lineage can stay on the phone if you prefer.

Questions

Does it work offline?

Yes. Everything is kept on the phone first, so you can log, scan and print in a basement or a grow tent with no signal. If you use the cloud, it syncs when you are back online.

Where is my data kept?

You choose in Settings, Privacy & security: only on the phone, on the phone and in the cloud (stored in the EU, Ireland), or on the phone with an encrypted cloud backup that only your passphrase or recovery key can open. Details are in the privacy policy.

What does it cost?

Spora is free during early access. If that changes, you will hear about it in the app well before, and exporting all your records to Excel stays free either way.

When is it coming to iPhone?

Spora is in App Store review now. Send a short email with the subject "Spora iPhone" and you will hear from us the day it is out. Nothing else, and no list to leave.

Can I mark sectors on a photo of my plate?

Yes. Take the photo with the dish inside the guide circle, or use one you already have. Draw colonies as circles and sectors as dashed wedges, and Spora suggests which colony a sector sits in. The marks stay in place on any phone. Prefer no photo? List the regions by letter instead.

Does a sector mean a pure culture?

No, and Spora never says so. A colony or sector is recorded as a place on a plate with what you saw. A daughter culture taken from it is a candidate isolate, and stays one until you record real evidence. Branch comparisons show only what was recorded, with the number of batches behind each figure. Fast growth or a rhizomorphic look is noted, not treated as proof.

Which label printers?

Small Bluetooth label printers print straight from the phone, down to 30 by 20 millimetres. Spora is tested on the Phomemo M221 and also speaks to NIIMBOT printers. For any other printer, print an A4 sheet of labels from a PDF.

Do I need labels to log a harvest?

No. Scan a label if you have one. Otherwise pick the batch from a list, or type the code you wrote on the jar or bag with a marker.

Coming from another app or a spreadsheet?

Bring your records with you. Export them from your old grow app or save your spreadsheet as CSV or Excel, and Spora rebuilds your strains, batches, lineage and harvests. You see everything before it is saved, and you can undo the import.

Which languages?

English, Norwegian, Swedish, Danish, German, Spanish, French, Italian, Polish and Russian. You choose one on the very first screen when you open Spora, and you can change it in Settings later.

Can I delete my account?

Yes, from inside the app under Settings, and it takes effect straight away. If you cannot open the app, email us and we delete it within 30 days. What gets removed.
